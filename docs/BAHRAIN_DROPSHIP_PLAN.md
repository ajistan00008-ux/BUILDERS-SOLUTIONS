# DAILY.BH — operating plan

A Bahraini shelf of everyday beauty essentials for men and women, carrying almost no
stock of its own, supplied by brands that send samples first.

Live, interactive version at `/daily/plan` (gated — see Access below). Every figure
comes from `content/daily.ts`, which also prices the shop, so the two cannot drift.

**Reference figures checked September–October 2026. Every one is an estimate until a
signed quote or agreement sits behind it.**

---

## 0. The one decision that matters

Three ways to run this. Only one survives contact with Bahraini customs.

| | Cross-border dropship | **Consignment (local dropship)** | Buy and hold |
|---|---|---|---|
| How | Brand ships one parcel direct to the customer | **Brand ships one case to our Manama 3PL and keeps title** | We buy a pallet outright |
| Capital | None | **Near zero — pay on sale** | BHD 8,000–15,000 per cycle |
| Customer waits | 10–21 days | **Same day in Manama** | Same day |
| Logistics per order | ≈ BHD 8.500 | **≈ BHD 1.180** | ≈ BHD 1.180 |
| Gross margin | Negative on most orders | **55–65%** | 62–70% |
| NHRA | **Not satisfied** | **Satisfied — one notification per SKU** | Satisfied |
| Verdict | ❌ Does not work here | ✅ **Build on this** | ⏳ Later, per SKU |

### Why cross-border dropship fails in Bahrain specifically

Two hard walls, neither of which is a margin problem you can optimise away.

**Cost.** A BHD 11.500 basket shipped as one parcel from India or China carries roughly
BHD 6–7 of courier, BHD 0.60 of duty and VAT, and a per-parcel clearing fee. You pay
more to move the goods than the goods are worth.

**Regulation, and this is the serious one.** A cosmetic may not be placed on the
Bahraini market without an NHRA notification. A parcel shipped direct from an
unregistered foreign seller to a consumer does not have one. Customs can hold or return
it, and because we took the order and the money, the exposure is ours — not the
supplier's. At a hundred orders a week that stops being inefficiency and becomes a
regulatory problem with our name on it.

### What consignment keeps from the dropship idea

Everything that actually mattered: no inventory on the balance sheet, goods paid for
only after they sell, stock risk with the brand, and products listed without buying them
first. The only thing given up is shipping each order individually from abroad — which
was never an advantage.

## 1. The NHRA gate

Six to eight working weeks per SKU, and nothing sells before it clears. This is the
schedule, so build around it.

| What | Who | Time | Rough cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| CR with import/export + retail activity | MOIC (Sijilat) | 1–2 weeks | BHD 50–300 |
| Customs importer code (OFOQ) | Bahrain Customs | Days | Nominal |
| VAT registration | NBR | 1–2 weeks | Free |
| **Cosmetic product notification, per SKU** | **NHRA** | **30–40 working days** | Per-SKU fee |
| Arabic label / artwork approval | NHRA + MOIC | With the above | Artwork cost |

**File in waves.** The six-to-eight weeks runs in parallel across SKUs, so twelve
notifications cost the same elapsed time as one. Hold samples until a wave is ready.

**Register for VAT voluntarily.** Mandatory above BHD 37,500 turnover, voluntary above
BHD 18,750. It turns import VAT from a cost into reclaimable input tax.

### Two categories to refuse at the door

- **Medical claims.** Anything presenting as treating hair loss, acne or a skin
  condition — including anything containing minoxidil — is a pharmaceutical. Different
  regulator, and such a claim gets a cosmetic filing rejected outright.
- **Skin lightening.** Whatever it is called on the pack, it carries real regulatory and
  reputational risk across the GCC. It is also lucrative, which is exactly why the
  decision to stay out should be written down now rather than argued later.

## 2. Getting brands to send samples

Free product is earned, not asked for. A brand seeds a channel that offers an audience,
a market it cannot reach, or a decision it needs made cheaply. We lead with the last
two: a Bahraini entity that will handle NHRA, customs and shelf placement is worth more
to a mid-size Indian or Turkish brand than another reviewer.

| Source | Volume | Conversion | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| Indian D2C brands already exporting to GCC | ~200 reachable | Medium | Cold email + line card |
| Beautyworld Middle East (Dubai, annual) | 1,500+ exhibitors | High | 3 days, ~BHD 400 all-in |
| Turkish / Korean export agents | ~50 agencies | High per contact | One agent opens twenty brands |
| GCC distributors wanting a Bahrain sub-channel | ~30 | Medium | They already hold NHRA files |
| Inbound via `/daily/partners` | Slow at first | Highest | Compounds once the shelf is visible |

**Target: 40 brands contacted, 12 sending samples, 5 stocked in the first 120 days.**

The pitch that works is not "we will promote you" — every brand hears that daily. It is:
*we will tell you whether Bahrain is worth your time, for the cost of two units, and if
it is, we do the regulatory work.* That is a cheap option on a new market.

**Disclosure is non-negotiable.** Every product supplied free carries a visible line
saying so. Beyond advertising standards, a shelf where every verdict is positive is a
shelf nobody believes — which destroys the thing brands are paying attention to. The
refusals are the asset.

## 3. Consignment terms worth signing

1. **Title passes on sale, not delivery.** Stock sits in our 3PL; we invoice monthly on
   units sold.
2. **Consignment price fixed 12 months, quoted in USD.** BHD is pegged to USD, so this
   removes FX risk entirely and leaves it with them.
3. **Minimum 18 months residual shelf life on arrival**, with a written rule for stock
   inside six months — return at their cost, or markdown by agreement.
4. **They supply the regulatory pack:** full ingredient breakdown, GMP certificate,
   free-sale certificate, artwork files adaptable for Arabic.
5. **An authorisation letter naming our Bahraini entity** as authorised representative.
   Without it there is no NHRA filing.
6. **Either side exits on 60 days**, unsold stock returned at their cost or bought out at
   consignment price at our option.

**When to stop consigning.** Once a SKU has three months of steady re-orders, buying it
outright adds roughly 8 points of gross margin. Do that per-SKU on proven winners only —
the consignment shelf exists so unproven products cost nothing to carry.

## 4. Inbound freight and clearing

One clearance per shipment, not one per order. This is the entire cost advantage.

| Lane | Rate | Per unit (≈250 g) | Transit | Use for |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| Courier (DHL/FedEx, <30 kg) | ₹950–2,300/kg | ~BHD 0.85 | 2–4 days | Samples, NHRA dossier |
| Air consolidated (50–100 kg) | ₹700–900/kg | ~BHD 0.53 | 4–7 days | First case, stock-outs |
| **Sea LCL (1 CBM+)** | ~USD 550–650/CBM | **~BHD 0.09** | 18–28 days | **Every repeat case** |
| Sea FCL 20ft | USD 1,400–2,200 | ~BHD 0.03 | 18–28 days | Above 2,000 units/month |

Duty is 5% on CIF for HS Chapter 33; import VAT is 10% on CIF plus duty, reclaimable
once registered. Get a binding classification ruling on the first shipment.

Door-to-door lead time on the sea lane is four to six weeks, so you always order against
demand you have not seen. Hold eight weeks of cover, re-order at four — easier under
consignment, because the cover costs the brand, not us.

## 5. Warehouse and last mile

- **Air-conditioned storage is not optional.** Emulsions split and sticks slump above
  ~40°C. Losing a batch is bad; losing *someone else's consigned* batch is the fastest
  way to lose a brand.
- **Start with a 3PL.** Pick-pack ~BHD 0.18/order plus storage. Scales without a lease.
- **Batch and expiry at goods-in, FEFO picking, monthly count.** A recall you cannot
  trace to a batch is existential, and consigned stock is someone else's liability.
- **Last mile from BHD 1.000** on a local 3PL contract, ~BHD 0.400 once an own driver
  runs 25+ batched drops a day. Offer cash on delivery from day one.

### The free-delivery line

Set at BHD 8.000. Last mile costs about a dinar an order whatever is in the box — at a
BHD 2 basket that is most of the revenue; at BHD 11.500 it is under a tenth. A broad
shelf across five categories is not merchandising ambition, it is the mechanism that
makes delivery survivable.

**Known cliff:** a BHD 7.950 basket contributes BHD 3.706; a BHD 8.050 basket contributes
BHD 2.878, and contribution only recovers past about BHD 9.600. There is a ~1.6 dinar
dead zone just above the line. Accepted because the bundles sit at 7.950, 11.450 and
11.950 — deliberately either side of it. If the dead zone fills up, move the line to BHD
9.500 before touching shelf prices.

## 6. Catalogue and pricing

37 SKUs across five categories, men and women. 30 stocked, 7 in the pipeline.

**One rule: no single unit above BHD 5.** The ceiling is the positioning. The moment a
BHD 9 serum appears, the shelf stops being "where daily essentials are sensibly priced"
and becomes another beauty shop competing on range — which we lose. Bundles may exceed
BHD 5; single units may not.

| Tier | Band | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Hooks | BHD 0.950–1.950 | Lip balm, hand cream, deodorant, rosemary 100 ml — get the first order |
| Core | BHD 2.250–3.450 | The re-ordered middle: cleansers, washes, lotions, beard oil |
| Ceiling | BHD 3.950–4.450 | Serums and SPF — highest margin, slowest re-order |
| Bundles | BHD 4.950–11.950 | Cross the free-delivery line, set the routine |

Blended gross margin across the 34 single SKUs: **60.5%**. Thinnest is the vitamin C
serum at 58.0%; no SKU is negative.

Hold headline prices publicly and discount through bundles and subscription, never the
shelf price. Once BHD 1.750 has been advertised, BHD 1.950 reads as an increase forever.

## 7. Unit economics

| Basket | Contribution | % of net | After BHD 1.800 CAC |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1.950 | 0.622 | 35.1% | −1.178 |
| 4.950 (sampler) | 2.164 | 48.1% | 0.364 |
| 7.950 (hair kit) | 3.706 | 51.3% | 1.906 |
| 11.500 (target AOV) | 4.651 | 44.5% | 2.851 |
| 11.500, own driver | 5.251 | 50.2% | 3.451 |

A single BHD 1.950 bottle does not pay for its own acquisition — it pays back on the
second order. Everything on the site that lifts the basket exists for this reason.

## 9. The five risks

1. **NHRA timing.** Six to eight weeks per wave, gating all revenue. File wave one
   before the storefront is finished, not after.
2. **Brand concentration.** If three brands are 70% of the shelf, one leaving is an
   extinction event. No brand above 25% of revenue past month six.
3. **Consigned stock damage.** Heat, breakage or a mis-pick destroys someone else's
   property. Insure the warehoused value; agree the liability split in writing before
   the first case ships.
4. **Grey-market undercutting.** Someone will fly product in as luggage and sell it on
   Instagram with no VAT and no notification. You beat that on next-day delivery, a real
   invoice, returns and traceability — not on price.
5. **Breadth without depth.** 37 SKUs is a lot to photograph, describe, notify and
   forecast. Better twelve that re-order than forty that sit. Cut anything with no
   second order by month four.

## 10. First 120 days

| Weeks | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | CR with import + retail. Customs code, VAT registration. Build the 40-brand target list. |
| 3–5 | First outreach wave. Samples arrive. Begin the 21-day protocol — including 45°C for 14 days. |
| 6–8 | Verdicts out to every brand, including refusals. Sign first three consignment agreements. Appoint authorised representative. |
| 9–10 | File NHRA wave one (10–12 SKUs). Approve Arabic artwork. Sign 3PL and last-mile contracts. |
| 11–14 | Notification clears. First cases by air, repeats by sea. Photograph everything in Manama; replace the drawn pack art. |
| 15–17 | Soft launch to a WhatsApp list plus a small paid test. Watch basket size and attachment, not traffic. |
| 18+ | Outreach wave two off a live shelf — far easier. Convert top three SKUs from consignment to owned stock. |

**The one metric:** contribution per order after delivery. Above BHD 1.800 you can buy
customers profitably. Below BHD 0.900 the basket is too small and traffic will not fix it.

## Implementation notes

- Catalogue, costs, fulfilment models and partner copy all live in `content/daily.ts`.
  The storefront and the plan page both read it; there is no second copy.
- `partnerStatus` on a product is the single switch controlling whether it can be
  bought. Only set `stocked` once NHRA notification is through and units are physically
  in the 3PL.
- Brand names other than **Alps Goodness** are placeholder house labels standing in for
  partner slots — invented, not real companies. The `/daily/partners` board says so.
- Pack illustrations in `components/store/ProductArt.tsx` are drawn, not photographed.
  Replace with studio shots as each brand signs.
- `WHATSAPP_NUMBER` in `CheckoutForm.tsx` and `Sections.tsx`, and the footer link in
  `StoreChrome.tsx`, are placeholders. Set them before launch.
- Checkout and the partner form both hand off to WhatsApp. Replace with real endpoints
  once a gateway (BenefitPay / Credimax) and a CRM are live.
- `/rosemary/*` permanently redirects to `/daily/*` — the old preview link was shared.
