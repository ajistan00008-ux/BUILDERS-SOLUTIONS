# ROSEMARY.BH — sourcing rosemary water in India, selling it in Bahrain

Operating plan behind the storefront at `/rosemary`. The live, interactive version of
the numbers is at `/rosemary/plan`; every figure there is computed from
`content/rosemary.ts`, so change the constants in that file and both this plan and the
shop prices move together.

**Reference figures checked September 2026. Every one is an estimate until you hold a
signed quote against it.**

---

## 0. The target, in one line

Land the 100 ml rosemary water spray in a Bahraini customer's hand for **BHD 1.950
including VAT** — roughly half the nearest Gulf shelf price — and still keep a 67%
gross margin. It works, but only on one specific configuration of the supply chain.

| SKU | Our price (VAT incl.) | Nearest Gulf shelf | Undercut | Landed cost | Gross margin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 ml spray | BHD 1.950 | BHD 3.670 (Kuwait) | −47% | BHD 0.577 | 67.5% |
| 200 ml spray | BHD 2.950 | BHD 4.620 (UAE) | −36% | BHD 1.030 | 61.6% |
| 305 ml refill | BHD 3.750 | BHD 6.060 (UAE, 475 ml) | −38% | BHD 1.169 | 65.7% |
| Spray + refill duo | BHD 5.250 | — | free delivery | BHD 1.746 | 63.4% |
| Growth ritual kit | BHD 6.500 | — | free delivery | BHD 2.175 | 63.2% |
| Refill saver twin | BHD 6.950 | — | free delivery | BHD 2.338 | 63.0% |

Assumptions: FOB at 50% off Indian MRP, sea LCL inbound, FX 254 INR/BHD, 5% duty,
10% VAT stripped out of the shelf price.

## 1. Supply — who to buy from

The product is **Alps Goodness Rosemary Water**. Brand owner: **Manash Lifestyle Pvt.
Ltd.**, Mumbai. Manufacturer: **Naturis Cosmetics Pvt. Ltd.**, Samba, J&K. Approach
the brand owner's export desk — the factory is a contract manufacturer and cannot sell
you the brand.

| Route | Price per 100 ml | Lead time | Verdict |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Retail arbitrage (Nykaa, Amazon.in, Purplle) | ₹199–207 | 3–5 days | Samples only. Never stock. |
| Indian wholesaler / super-stockist | ₹135–150 | 1–2 weeks | Workable fallback, ~35% off MRP |
| **Brand owner direct, export terms** | **₹105–125** | 3–5 weeks | **The target, 45–55% off MRP** |

Indian MRP is ₹230 for the 100 ml, ₹399 for the 200 ml, ~₹425 for the 305 ml refill.

### What to get into the supply agreement, in order of value

1. **Exclusive Bahrain distribution, 24 months**, against a minimum annual volume.
   Without it, your own success invites the next importer next year.
2. **Ex-works price at 45–55% off MRP, quoted in USD, fixed 12 months.** BHD is pegged
   to USD, so pricing in USD removes the FX risk entirely and leaves it with them.
3. **Export documentation per shipment**: commercial invoice, packing list, certificate
   of origin, free-sale certificate, GMP certificate, full ingredient breakdown,
   Bahrain-compliant artwork file.
4. **Minimum 18 months residual shelf life on arrival**, batch codes legible after the
   sea lane.
5. **An authorisation letter naming your Bahrain entity as authorised importer.** You
   cannot register the product without it — get it before any deposit leaves.

**Payment terms:** first order 50% advance / 50% against scan of the bill of lading.
From order three, 30/70 or a documentary LC at sight. Never 100% in advance to a
supplier that has not yet delivered to you.

**On the Indian side:** IEC code, AD code registered at the port, and a **Letter of
Undertaking so exports are zero-rated for GST** — otherwise 18% GST sits in your cost
until you claw it back. If the supplier ships FOB they handle the shipping bill; if you
buy ex-works you need an Indian forwarder to do it.

## 2. Compliance in Bahrain — start this on day one

| What | Authority | Time | Rough cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| CR with import/export + retail activity | MOIC (Sijilat) | 1–2 weeks | BHD 50–300 |
| Customs importer code (OFOQ) | Bahrain Customs | days | nominal |
| VAT registration | NBR | 1–2 weeks | free |
| **Cosmetic product notification** | **NHRA** | **30–40 working days** | per-SKU fee |
| Arabic label / artwork approval | NHRA + MOIC | with the above | artwork cost |

**NHRA is the long pole.** Cosmetics must be notified before they can be cleared or
sold; the application goes through a Bahrain-established authorised representative and
takes six to eight working weeks. File the moment you have the authorisation letter and
the ingredient breakdown — do not wait for the supply agreement to be signed.

**Register for VAT voluntarily from the start.** Mandatory above BHD 37,500 turnover,
voluntary above BHD 18,750. Registering turns the 10% import VAT from a cost into
reclaimable input tax — about 6 fils on every 100 ml bottle.

> ⚠️ **Fix the claims before the label goes to NHRA.** Some third-party marketplace
> listings of this product carry a "20% minoxidil" line. That is a listing error, not
> the formulation — but a cosmetic notification carrying a drug claim gets rejected, and
> a drug claim on a Bahraini shelf is a regulatory problem, not a marketing one. Keep
> every claim cosmetic: appearance of thickness, scalp freshness, frizz. No "treats hair
> loss", no "minoxidil", no medical before-and-after framing.

## 3. Freight — the single decision that decides the business

| Lane | Rate | Per 100 ml unit | Transit | Use for |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| Courier (DHL/FedEx, <30 kg) | ₹950–2,300/kg | ~BHD 0.85 | 2–4 days | Samples, NHRA dossier, emergencies |
| Air consolidated (50–100 kg) | ₹700–900/kg | ~BHD 0.53 | 4–7 days | Launch stock, stock-outs |
| **Sea LCL (1 CBM+)** | **~USD 550–650/CBM all-in** | **~BHD 0.094** | 18–28 days | **Every repeat order** |
| Sea FCL 20ft | USD 1,400–2,200 | ~BHD 0.03 | 18–28 days | Once you sell 2,000+ units/month |

All-in figures include origin charges, destination THC, clearance and delivery to a
Manama warehouse. One CBM holds roughly 2,500 × 100 ml bottles.

**Air costs about 530 fils a bottle; sea costs about 9.** On the air lane the blended
gross margin collapses from 65% to 35% and the BHD 1.950 price stops working.

**So split the plan:** fly the first 60–80 kg so the shop can open and NHRA has physical
samples, then put everything after that on the water. Route: Nhava Sheva or Mundra →
Khalifa Bin Salman Port, consolidated by an Indian forwarder with a Bahraini clearing
agent at the other end.

Lead time is four to six weeks door to door, so you are always ordering against demand
you have not seen. **Hold eight weeks of cover, re-order at four.** A 24-month shelf
life forgives over-ordering far more cheaply than a stock-out costs you.

## 4. What the border takes

| Line | Basis | Rate | On a 100 ml bottle |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Customs duty | CIF value | 5% | BHD 0.027 |
| Import VAT | CIF + duty | 10% | BHD 0.060 — reclaimable |
| Clearance & handling | per consignment | — | under 1 fils at volume |

HS Chapter 33 (3305.90 / 3307.90 depending on presentation). **Get a binding
classification ruling from Bahrain Customs on the first shipment** so it never moves.

Duty is a real cost. Import VAT is not, once registered — you pay it at the border,
reclaim it on the return, and remit the 10% you charge the customer.

## 5. Warehousing

Bahrain is 780 km². You do not need a distribution network; you need one air-conditioned
room.

- **Start:** 20–30 m² of racked, **air-conditioned** storage in Sitra or Tubli, roughly
  BHD 80–150/month. Hydrosol degrades in heat — a non-conditioned unit in a Bahraini
  August will cost you the batch.
- **Scale:** move to a 3PL doing pick-pack and courier handover, around BHD 0.12–0.20
  per order.
- **Discipline:** FEFO picking, batch and expiry logged at goods-in, monthly count. A
  cosmetics recall you cannot trace to a batch is existential, not inconvenient.

## 6. Last mile — about a dinar an order, whatever is in the box

| Option | Cost per order | Speed | COD |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Local Bahraini 3PL (Oreem, Wasel, J&T) | from BHD 1.000 | same day | yes, settled in BHD |
| Aramex Bahrain domestic | BHD 1.200–2.000 | next day | yes |
| Bahrain Post local parcel (≤2 kg) | BHD 1.200 | 2–3 days | no |
| Own driver, batched routes | ~BHD 0.400 at 25+ drops/day | same day | yes |

Start with a local 3PL on a monthly contract. **Offer cash on delivery from day one** —
still around an eighth of Bahraini e-commerce payments, and it removes the trust
objection for a first-time buyer of a cheap import. BenefitPay next, cards third.

### The uncomfortable arithmetic of a BHD 1.950 order

| Scenario | Contribution per order |
|---|---:|
| One 100 ml bottle, delivery free | **−BHD 0.149** |
| One 100 ml bottle, BHD 0.900 delivery fee | BHD 0.732 |
| Basket BHD 5.200, delivery free | BHD 1.653 |
| Refill twin BHD 6.950, delivery free | BHD 2.624 |

Against a target acquisition cost of BHD 1.200, only baskets above roughly BHD 2.900
pay for their own first order. **BHD 1.950 is an acquisition price, not a profit
centre** — it pays back on the second order. Every merchandising decision on the site
(bundles, refill twin, subscription, the BHD 5.000 free-delivery line) exists to lift
the basket. Do not let anyone quietly make delivery free on a single bottle.

**Known 13-fils cliff:** a BHD 3.900 basket contributes BHD 1.813; a BHD 5.250 basket
contributes BHD 1.681, because crossing BHD 5.000 hands us the whole dinar of delivery.
The line only breaks even again around BHD 5.500. Keep the threshold at 5.000 anyway —
almost no real basket lands at 3.900 (the natural steps are 1.950, 2.950 and 4.900, all
of which improve sharply when pulled over). If contribution tightens, **raise the
threshold to BHD 5.500 before touching the shelf price.**

## 7. Pricing policy

Hold the BHD 1.950 line publicly. Discount through bundles and subscription, never
through the headline price: once you have advertised BHD 1.750, BHD 1.950 reads as an
increase forever.

The ladder does three jobs at once — it gives a price nobody in the region can advertise
against, it makes the refill (highest margin, lowest freight, most repeatable) the
obviously rational buy, and it puts the free-delivery line exactly where a second item
has to join the basket.

## 9. The five risks worth writing down

1. **NHRA delay.** Six to eight weeks, and it gates everything. File first, negotiate
   second. Do not let stock arrive before the notification is in.
2. **Losing distribution.** Without exclusivity you are renting the market. 24 months in
   writing, or accept that.
3. **Grey-market undercutting.** Someone will fly bottles in as personal luggage and sell
   them on Instagram at BHD 1.500 with no VAT and no duty. You cannot beat that on
   price — you beat it on same-day delivery, a real invoice, returns, and batch
   traceability. Say so on the site.
4. **Heat.** Non-conditioned storage or an uninsulated van in August degrades hydrosol.
   Condition the warehouse; do not let a 3PL leave stock on a loading bay.
5. **Freight shock.** Gulf and Red Sea routing disruption has doubled container rates
   twice in recent years. Keep eight weeks of cover and price in USD so only one
   variable can move at a time.

## 10. First ninety days

| Weeks | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Incorporate / extend CR with import + retail. Approach the brand owner. Order retail samples for photography and the dossier. |
| 3–4 | Heads of terms and the authorisation letter. Appoint the Bahrain authorised representative. Register for VAT and the customs importer code. |
| 5–6 | File the NHRA cosmetic notification. Sign the supply agreement. Place PO #1. Approve Arabic artwork. |
| 7–9 | Air the launch 80 kg; book the LCL balance. Sign warehouse and 3PL. Shoot real product photography in Manama and replace the drawn artwork. |
| 10–12 | NHRA clears. Stock lands and is counted in. Soft launch to a WhatsApp list plus a small paid test. Watch basket size, not traffic. |
| 13+ | Re-order on the sea lane before you think you need to. Launch subscription on the 305 ml refill. Only then widen the range. |

## The one metric to run the business on

Not revenue, not traffic — **contribution per order after delivery**. Above BHD 1.200
you can buy customers profitably and grow as fast as working capital allows. Below
BHD 0.600 the basket is too small and no amount of traffic will fix it.
